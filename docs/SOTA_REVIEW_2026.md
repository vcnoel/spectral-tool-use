# State of the art, 2025 – October 2026: detecting wrong or hallucinated tool calls made by LLM agents

Compiled 2026-10-05 from primary sources (arXiv abstract/HTML pages, ACL Anthology, PMLR, Hugging Face dataset cards, GitHub READMEs/LICENSE files). Every factual claim below was read from the cited page; anything I could not confirm on a primary page is tagged **[unverified]**. Numbers are quoted as reported; I did not re-derive them. Our paper's identity (attention-based readouts: per-head spectral profiles and anchored span readouts, compared with and combined with hidden-state probes, on held-out tools) is used only to decide what to look for; no text from our manuscripts was used in any query.

Legend for tables: HO-tools = evaluation tools/APIs disjoint from training tools; CI = any uncertainty reported on the headline detection metric (fold std, bootstrap, ±σ); Code = public code or data at time of checking.

---

## 0. Executive summary

1. **A sub-field now exists.** Since January 2026 at least eleven arXiv papers detect tool-call errors from a model's own internals: hidden-state linear probes at the last token or at argument positions (Healy et al. 2601.05214; Yeats et al. 2608.27750; Yu et al. 2608.03071; Sun et al. 2605.09252; Wu et al. 2605.00737), sparse MLP-neuron detectors (Ke et al. 2608.00218), a concurrent LoRA "latent critic" (Vijayvargiya & Lokesh 2608.10430), SAE features (Tatsat & Shater 2605.06890), per-tool activation means / logit-gap flags (Wu et al. 2605.07990, ICML 2026), and one attention-based account (Chen 2606.16364, "Looking Is Not Picking", attention mass on tool-definition segments). Reported AUROCs cluster at **0.80–0.90 for argument-validity / wrong-call detection** and **0.90–1.00 for over-calling / missing-call detection**, almost always on single-turn function calling with AST-matched labels.
2. **Attention-based and spectral detectors are mature in QA hallucination but have not been ported to tool calls.** Lookback Lens (EMNLP 2024), LLM-Check (NeurIPS 2024), LapEigvals (EMNLP 2025, 2502.17598), SinkProbe (ICML 2026, 2604.10697), CHARM (ICLR 2026, 2509.24770), RAUQ (ICML 2026, 2505.20045) and AggTruth (ICCS 2025) are all evaluated on QA/summarisation/math; none reports a function-calling result. The single attention result on tool calls (Chen 2606.16364) uses a scalar attention-margin averaged over all layers and heads (AUROC 0.893 on 198 real BFCL failures) and does not look at spectra, per-head profiles, or sinks.
3. **Confidence-based baselines are weak on function calling.** Ye et al. 2604.22985 (first systematic UQ study for function calling, 8 open models 0.5B–12B on BFCL): best single-sample score G-NLL AUROC about 0.76–0.78 averaged over models; semantic entropy adaptations do not beat it; average token probability in function-call outputs is 0.97, which explains why sampling-based UQ is uninformative. On multi-turn (BFCL-v4 multi-turn, τ²-bench) white-box scores reach at most 0.725 AUROC and black-box consistency up to 0.868 (Bouchard & Chauhan 2608.11552).
4. **Output-only judges are measurably unreliable on tool traces.** Substring judges reach κ≈0.05 and LLM judges κ≈0.43–0.57 against humans (Gurram 2604.16706); on hard queries without ground truth six LLM judges converge to 77–82 % alignment regardless of size (AgentJudgeBench 2608.26623, EMNLP 2026); outcome-only judges catch 45 % of "silent" faults while flagging 33 % of correct trajectories (2609.00038).
5. **Held-out tools are almost never enforced.** Only ParamBench (2608.03071; 24 held-out APIs out of 81) and the Latent Critic (2608.10430; 500 test tasks with unseen tools, plus 200 ToolAlpaca OOD) split by tool. Yeats et al. test label-space transfer (error-type disjoint), PRISMS tests prompt-level disjointness only, Healy et al. report no transfer. **No paper compares attention-only readouts with hidden-state probes, or studies their complementarity, on tool calls.** This is the gap.
6. **Benchmarks a 2026 reviewer will expect:** BFCL (v3/v4 single-turn AST categories; by far the most used; Apache 2.0; ground-truth calls plus AST checker), one of the verified-call training corpora used by Healy/PRISMS (xLAM-60k CC-BY-4.0 with 3,673 executable APIs, or Glaive-v2 Apache 2.0) because they give automatic labels and allow tool-disjoint splits, and τ²-bench (MIT; gold action sequences and DB-state checker) if a multi-turn result is wanted. For the "unavailable-tool / should-not-call" trap the standard sets are BFCL irrelevance/live_irrelevance, When2Call (NAACL 2025, CC BY 4.0, 3,652 MCQ test items) and SimpleToolHalluBench (296 pairs; ACL 2026). "ToolHallu" as a named dataset could not be found **[unverified / probably not a real name]**.

Relevance for us: LapEigvals and SinkProbe are the two published spectral/sink readouts with code; both are **symmetric-spectrum** (Laplacian eigenvalues) or **column-mass** (sink) statistics and both report *within-dataset* 5-fold AUROC only; LapEigvals explicitly shows poor cross-dataset transfer. Dahlem et al. give a theoretical reason to expect symmetric spectra to miss directional information, which is an argument for anchored (query-span → key-span) readouts. None of these has been run on function-calling outputs, where generated spans are short, highly structured, and ≈0.97 average token probability (Ye et al.).

## 3. Recommendation

### 3.1 Benchmarks a 2026 reviewer will expect

1. **BFCL (v3/v4 single-turn AST categories: simple, multiple, parallel, parallel_multiple, plus live_multiple and irrelevance/live_irrelevance).** Rationale: 7 of the 2025–26 detection/UQ papers in Section 1 use it (Yeats, Ye, Bouchard, Chen, Yu, TRUST, ToolPRMBench); Apache 2.0; gold calls plus a deterministic AST checker produce wrong-call labels at zero annotation cost; the irrelevance categories give the should-abstain trap for free; items fit a 4B model on 16 GB for the non-live categories. Held-out tools: group by function name (simple/multiple) or by the tool set of a live_multiple item and split by group; report both random-split and tool-disjoint AUROC.
2. **xLAM-function-calling-60k (CC-BY-4.0, 3,673 executable APIs), optionally with Glaive-v2 and ToolACE for cross-corpus checks.** Rationale: this is where Healy et al. and PRISMS get their validity labels (model rollouts against verified references, AST + normalised value match); it supplies thousands of natural errors per model (PRISMS: ≈15k calls, invalid rate 0.10–0.49 by source for Qwen3-4B), it has enough distinct APIs for a genuine **API-disjoint split**, and it supports the parallel-call "missing call" category. Prefer xLAM over Glaive as the headline because its references are execution-verified.
3. **τ²-bench (MIT; retail 115 / airline 50 / telecom 114) for one multi-turn result** if the A100 budget allows; this is what Bouchard & Chauhan and Chen use, gold action sequences exist, and the 2026 UQ literature shows white-box scores collapse there (≤0.725), so an attention readout that holds up on even one domain is a strong result. If budget is tight, substitute BFCL multi_turn_base (200) plus multi_turn_miss_func/miss_param, which also give missing-function/missing-parameter traps and are labelled by the official checker.

**Adversarial / unavailable-tool set.** The literature's standard is not one dataset but a trio: BFCL irrelevance (875 live + 240 non-live), **When2Call** (NAACL 2025, CC BY 4.0, 3,652 MCQ test items with "cannot answer" and "ask" classes), and **SimpleToolHalluBench** (296 NTA/DT pairs; ACL 2026). When2Call is the one with a licence, an HF card and a 2026 user (TRUST). SimpleToolHalluBench is small and licence-unverified but is the current "distractor tool" reference. Optional security flavour: AgentDojo (MIT) injection cases, though no 2025–26 detection paper uses it.

### 3.3 Baselines to reimplement (strongest 2–3, with reference code)

1. **Hidden-state linear probe** in the three published variants: last-token, mid-to-late layer, ℓ2 logistic (Yeats); three-position final-layer (function-name onset, mean-pooled argument span, closing delimiter; Healy); pre-parameter-value position at ≈2/3 depth (Yu). Reference code with a working feature-extraction + logistic-regression pipeline: github.com/Trustworthy-ML-Lab/when2tool (Qwen3 1.7B–32B, Llama 3.1/3.3 wrappers). PRISMS' sparse L1 variant is a cheap add-on once dense features exist.
2. **Token-level confidence**: G-NLL (greedy sequence NLL) and G-NLL-SMT (NLL over semantically meaningful tokens: function name and argument values only), MAX/AVG token probability, and **P(True)** prompting, exactly as in Ye et al. 2604.22985 (no code link found; the definitions are standard). Report smoothECE alongside AUROC as they do.
3. **Attention-based detectors with code**: LapEigvals (github.com/graphml-lab-pwr/lapeigvals; top-k Laplacian eigenvalues over all layers/heads, PCA-512, logistic regression), SinkProbe (github.com/graphml-lab-pwr/sink-probe; top-k sink scores over all layers/heads), Lookback Lens (github.com/voidism/Lookback-Lens; per-head context-vs-generation ratio), and LLM-Check attention-kernel log-det (github.com/GaurangSriramanan/LLM_Check_Hallucination_Detection). Plus the tool-specific **attention margin** of Chen 2606.16364 (gold-segment mass minus mean distractor mass, averaged over all layers and heads; no public code, ≈20 lines to reimplement). These four are the methods a reviewer will name when asked "why is your attention readout better than existing attention readouts".
4. Optional external judge: a rubric LLM judge (AgentJudgeBench-style structured rubric; +6.5 pp over free-form) and/or ToolPRM if released; both are output-only and cost a second model.

### 3.4 What the literature does not yet report (the gap)

- **Attention-only readouts vs hidden-state probes on tool calls.** No 2025–26 paper reports an attention-derived detector on function calling except Chen's single averaged scalar; none reports spectral or sink statistics on tool calls; SinkProbe/LapEigvals/CHARM/RAUQ are QA-only.
- **Per-head structure.** Every attention method above pools over all heads (or selects heads by a single criterion in RAUQ/AggTruth); per-head spectral profiles and which heads carry the signal are unreported for tool calls. Chen localises the causal effect to the deepest quarter of layers; Yin localises discriminability to late residual stream; nobody shows a head-level map.
- **Anchored (directional) readouts.** Dahlem et al. prove symmetric spectral diagnostics are orientation-blind; no detector uses query-span → key-span (e.g. generated-call → tool-schema / user-request) anchored statistics on tool calls beyond Chen's segment mass.
- **Complementarity.** No paper combines attention features with hidden-state features on tool calls or reports whether the errors they catch overlap (CHARM does it for QA; Latent Critic compares but does not combine).
- **Held-out-tool transfer** is reported only by ParamBench (24 APIs, parameter errors only) and Latent Critic (synthetic tools). Healy: none; Yeats: error-type transfer only; PRISMS: prompt-level only. Nobody reports held-out-tool AUROC for an attention method.
- **Per-failure-type analysis with intervals.** Yeats (7 types) and PRISMS (3) break down by type; Healy's 5-class taxonomy is reported in aggregate; nobody reports per-type AUROC for attention features, and intervals are inconsistently reported (none in Healy; deferred in PRISMS).
- **Small models.** The ≤4B regime is covered only by Yeats (gemma-3-1b, Phi-4-mini), PRISMS (Qwen3-1.7B, Gemma-3-4B), Ye (Qwen2.5-0.5B/3B, gemma-3-4b, Qwen3-4B), Wu (270M–27B), Latent Critic (Qwen3-4B); none with attention features. The 27B regime is covered by Wu (Gemma-3-27B steering), Tatsat (Gemma-3-27B SAEs) and Sun & Kazakov (Qwen3-32B); again no attention-based detection.
- **Mechanism.** The two mechanistic findings available are (i) the model usually attends to the right tool and still picks wrong, so the failure sits at the readout (Chen), and (ii) discriminability lives in late-layer residual streams, not in attention or MLP sub-blocks taken alone (Yin). A paper that shows which heads' spectra change on wrong calls and whether that is upstream or downstream of the residual signal would be new.
