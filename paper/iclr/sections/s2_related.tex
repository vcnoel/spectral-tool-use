\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hallucination in tool use.}
Tool-calling benchmarks measure whether a model can call the right function with the right arguments
\citep{patil2024gorilla,qin2024toolllm,patil2025bfcl}, and the current leaderboard adds a category in which no
tool applies and the model should abstain \citep{patil2025bfcl}. These evaluate capability; our question is
whether a failure can be detected from the model's own state before the call executes.
\citet{healy2026internal} first showed that it can, with a probe on residual-stream states at the
function-name, argument and delimiter positions; that probe is the residual-stream reference we compare
against. Concurrently, \citet{yeats2026calls} train linear probes on the hidden states of eighteen models on
the same benchmark and find they catch argument errors of the right type and wrong value and generalise to
unseen error kinds. That work establishes breadth on the residual tier; it has no attention tier, no length
floor and no held-out-tool split, which are the three things that decide the comparison made here.

\paragraph{Probing the residual stream.}
Supervised probes on hidden states detect factual error \citep{azaria2023internal,marks2024geometry}, predict
hallucination risk from the query alone \citep{ji2024internal}, and stand in for sampling-based estimates at
the cost of one pass \citep{kossen2024sep}; \citet{orgad2025know} showed the signal concentrates at the
exact-answer tokens, which is what the token-role probe applies to a call. The family also includes
covariance spectra \citep{chen2024inside}, real-time token-level probes \citep{obeso2025realtime},
cross-layer dynamics \citep{icrprobe2025}, hidden-layer trajectories \citep{li2025hsad} and claim-level probes
\citep{bhatnagar2026halt}, all requiring residual-stream access. Our contribution here is not a new probe but
the finding that the token-role probe remains the strongest single detector under a stricter protocol.

\paragraph{Reading attention.}
Attention is the tensor practitioners can most often read, used for cache eviction \citep{zhang2023h2o},
for hallucination through the ratio of attention to context against generation \citep{chuang2024lookback},
for multi-layer aggregation \citep{abnar2020quantifying} and for cross-depth consistency
\citep{badash2026layers}. The spectral line treats a layer as a graph or kernel: \citet{sriramanan2024llmcheck}
read eigenvalues of attention kernel maps, \citet{noel2025gsp} introduced Laplacian diagnostics of the
attention graph, and \citet{binkowski2025lapeigvals} obtained the strongest attention-only results to date
from top-$k$ Laplacian values; the same group has since read hallucination from attention sinks
\citep{binkowski2026sinks}, a per-head signal we do not evaluate here. \citet{dahlem2026transport} prove that
every transpose-invariant spectral diagnostic is blind to the direction of attention flow and evaluate
spectral detectors under length control; every spectrum in this paper is of a symmetrised graph and is
orientation-blind in their sense, which bounds what the attention tier can see and is why a length floor
sits in every table. Two features of this literature motivate Section~\ref{sec:theory}: nearly all of it
averages over heads before computing anything, and part of it computes quantities that need residual states
as a graph signal while presenting them as properties of attention. We tag every feature by the tensors it
consumes (Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}). Sampling-based signals such as semantic entropy and
self-consistency \citep{farquhar2024semantic,manakul2023selfcheckgpt} multiply inference cost and need the
freedom to sample, which an agent about to act does not have; we restrict the study to a single pass and
include summaries of the token log-probabilities as baselines rather than competitors.
