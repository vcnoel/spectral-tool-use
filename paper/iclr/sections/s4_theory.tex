\section{Why averaging heads destroys the signal}
\label{sec:theory}

Almost every attention-graph diagnostic averages the attention matrix over heads and reads a spectral
quantity off the graph that remains \citep{noel2025gsp,li2025hsad,badash2026layers}. The averaging step, not
the quantity read afterwards, decides whether the diagnostic can see a failure. Proofs are in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\paragraph{Setup.}
\label{sec:theory:setup}
Fix one layer with $H$ heads and $T$ positions. Head $h$ produces a row-stochastic, causal attention matrix
$A_h \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times T}$, $(A_h)_{ij}=0$ for $j>i$, symmetrised and stripped of self-loops,
\begin{equation}
W_h = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(A_h + A_h^{\top}\right) - \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(A_h + A_h^{\top})\right),
\label{eq:adj}
\end{equation}
with combinatorial Laplacian $L_h = D_h - W_h$, $D_h=\operatorname{diag}(W_h\mathbf{1})$, and symmetric
normalised Laplacian $\mathcal{L}_h = I - D_h^{-1/2}W_hD_h^{-1/2}$. The \emph{head-averaged} graph is
$\bar W = \frac1H\sum_h W_h$ with Laplacian $\bar L$; $\lamtwo$ is algebraic connectivity. A \emph{per-head}
read keeps $(\phi(W_1),\dots,\phi(W_H))$ for a scalar $\phi$; a \emph{head-averaged} read keeps $\phi(\bar W)$.

\begin{proposition}[annihilation]
\label{prop:annihilation}
Any function $\{W_h\}\mapsto\Phi(\bar W)$ of the head-averaged graph is constant on every set of head
configurations sharing the same mean. For every $H\ge 2$ and $T\ge 3$ there exist configurations
$\{W_h\}$, $\{W_h'\}$, each realisable by causal heads, with $\bar W=\bar W'$ and
$\max_h\lamtwo(L_h)-\max_h\lamtwo(L_h')=\Omega(1)$.
\end{proposition}
\label{sec:theory:annihilation}

The statement is elementary; we make it because the literature it addresses has behaved as though it were
false. It says nothing about which quantity is read, so it covers the Fiedler value, the whole eigenvalue
profile, Dirichlet energy and any statistic of the averaged graph a future paper might propose: what
distinguishes the two configurations is the \emph{contrast between heads}, and the mean is the statistic that
discards contrast. Whether real failures are visible in that contrast is empirical
(Section~\ref{sec:exp:perhead}); no averaged read can answer it either way.

\begin{proposition}[head averaging overstates connectivity]
\label{prop:jensen}
For any heads $W_1,\dots,W_H$,
\begin{equation}
\lamtwo(\bar L)\;\ge\;\frac1H\sum_{h=1}^{H}\lamtwo(L_h),
\label{eq:jensen}
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if the heads admit a common Fiedler vector.
\end{proposition}
\label{sec:theory:jensen}

The Laplacian is linear in the adjacency and $\lamtwo$ is a pointwise minimum of linear functionals, hence
concave, so Eq.~\ref{eq:jensen} is Jensen's inequality. The consequence is one-directional: a hallucinated
call that fragments the routing of a few heads lowers their $\lamtwo$, but the averaged graph is bounded
below by the mean over heads and its minimising direction is a consensus the fragmented heads need not share,
so the evidence of fragmentation is compressed toward that consensus. How far real layers sit from the
equality condition is measurable (Appendix~\ref{app:jensen}, Figure~\ref{fig:jensen}). On
\nJensenLayers{} layers from \nJensenModels{} models, Eq.~\ref{eq:jensen} holds with \combGapViolations{}
violations and a gap bounded away from zero, mean \combGapMean{}; the mean pairwise alignment of per-head
Fiedler vectors is \fiedlerAlignmentMean{}, far from the $1$ that equality needs. For the normalised
Laplacian the detectors use, the Rayleigh quotient is not linear in $W$ and Eq.~\ref{eq:jensen} is not a
theorem; empirically it holds on all but \normGapViolations{} layers, with the averaged graph reporting
connectivity a median \normRatioMedianPct{}\% above the mean over heads.

\paragraph{The strongest attention-only baseline reads degrees, not a spectrum.}
\label{sec:theory:lapeig}
LapEigvals \citep{binkowski2025lapeigvals} takes the top-$k$ eigenvalues of $L=D-A$ for the unsymmetrised
causal $A$, and its authors observe (their Lemma~1) that $L$ is lower triangular, so
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{spec}(L)=\{\,d_j-A_{jj}\,\}_{j\le T},
\label{eq:lapeigdiag}
\end{equation}
with $d$ their position-normalised in-degree; the same identity, read as sink score minus self-attention,
underlies \citet{binkowski2026sinks}. We take it as given and draw out what it implies.

\begin{remark}[LapEigvals is a degree statistic]
\label{prop:lapeig}
By Eq.~\ref{eq:lapeigdiag} the features depend on $A$ only through column sums and the diagonal: two causal
attention matrices with the same in-degree sequence and self-attention have identical features however
differently they route attention. They are a per-position degree statistic carrying no eigenvector
information, whereas the symmetrisation in Eq.~\ref{eq:adj} destroys triangularity and the spectra our
detectors read are global functionals of the routing.
\end{remark}

This is not a criticism of a method ours does not beat (Section~\ref{sec:exp:frontier}); it says the two
families are not two estimates of one object, and that keeping heads apart is the one thing they share.

\begin{corollary}[decorrelation prediction]
\label{cor:decorrelate}
If the two families measure different functionals, their rank correlation should be well below one, each
should retain signal once the other is conditioned on, and a combination should exceed both.
Section~\ref{sec:exp:decorrelate} tests the three; two hold and the third fails.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Length invariance by construction.}
\label{sec:theory:length}
Lengths differ systematically between correct and hallucinated calls, so a feature that scales with $T$ can
carry label information unrelated to routing. For any weighted graph with non-negative weights and no isolated
vertices, $\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal{L})\subset[0,2]$ whatever the number of nodes (Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded},
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), so every per-head feature is a function of a bounded spectrum whatever the call
length, whereas the combinatorial spectrum grows with $T$. Invariance by construction does not prove the
absence of a length confound, since routing may itself correlate with length; the floor in every table
measures it.
