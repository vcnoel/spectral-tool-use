\paragraph{Setup.}
\label{sec:exp:setup}
Table~\ref{tab:runs} lists the runs: five model families between 0.8B and 4B parameters, three data
distributions (the Glaive function-calling corpus \citep{glaive2024}, the curated single-turn categories of
BFCL-v4 \citep{patil2025bfcl} including the category where no tool applies, and its live categories,
contributed by users), and three attention designs (dense grouped-query attention at several head counts, a
hybrid stack in which a quarter of the layers compute softmax attention, and a sliding-window stack with four
query heads). Failure rates span an order of magnitude, from \rateQwenHybridGlaive{}\% to
\rateLlamaOneBBfcl{}\%, which separates claims about detectors from claims about how often models fail. The
full detector grid, with seed spreads and a common-population version, is in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

\subsection{The access frontier}
\label{sec:exp:frontier}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{fig2_frontier}
\caption{The access frontier. One representative per tier, fixed before any result was seen. (a) AUC on each
run's evaluated population; the grey bar runs from chance to the length floor. (b) Each representative's
margin over the floor with its paired 95\% interval (Appendix~\ref{app:stats}). The daggered run is
underpowered and drawn faded.}
\label{fig:frontier}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:frontier} is the deployment result, and it has two regimes. On the Llama, Qwen3 and Gemma
runs the internal tiers order stably: the residual probe leads, attention-only trails it by a few points, both
sit far above the floor, and the token log-probability reaches \aucLogprobLlamaThreeBBfclCall{} on Llama-3B
and \aucLogprobQwenDenseBfclCall{} on Qwen3-1.7B, which is chance, on a run where the same model's residual
stream supports \aucHiddenLRQwenDenseBfclCall{}. A guardrail built on confidence would pass exactly the calls
this paper is about on those models. On the MiniCPM5 and Qwen3.5 runs the picture inverts: log-probability
is the best single detector and no trained judge reaches it. Section~\ref{sec:exp:budget} examines that
division. Inside the internal tiers, on Llama-1B on Glaive the residual probe's paired margin over the
per-head profile is \dAucHiddenVsPerHeadBaseLlamaOneBGlaive{}
[\dAucHiddenVsPerHeadBaseLlamaOneBGlaiveLo{}, \dAucHiddenVsPerHeadBaseLlamaOneBGlaiveHi{}], and the two
attention judges differ by \dAucPerHeadVsLapEigBaseLlamaOneBGlaive{}
[\dAucPerHeadVsLapEigBaseLlamaOneBGlaiveLo{}, \dAucPerHeadVsLapEigBaseLlamaOneBGlaiveHi{}]; the full contrast
family per run is Table~\ref{tab:paired}, in which the residual probe leads the attention tier on every
powered run but one and per-head and LapEigvals trade places with intervals that span zero. We do not claim
to beat LapEigvals; the per-head profile offers compactness, $L\times H\times 5$ features against
$L\times H\times k$ with $k$ up to $100$, a graph-theoretic readout, and a small advantage at low label
budgets (Appendix~\ref{app:budget}). The detectors transfer across corpora and the floor does not: trained on
Glaive and applied to BFCL, the residual probe retains \transferHiddenLRLlamaThreeBGlaiveToLlamaThreeBBfcl{}
while the floor falls to \transferSurfaceConfoundLlamaThreeBGlaiveToLlamaThreeBBfcl{}
(Appendix~\ref{app:confound}).

\subsection{Whether a trained judge is needed is a property of the family}
\label{sec:exp:budget}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{fig6_family}
\caption{The advantage of the best residual probe over mean log-probability on every run, (a) on every
parseable call and (b) on the call-expected items, coloured by model family. The two groups do not overlap
on either population; for the recent families the sign of the effect depends on the population, the
separation between families does not.}
\label{fig:family}
\end{figure}

The division is complete rather than a tendency (Figure~\ref{fig:family}). Over \famNRunsSem{} runs scored
on every parseable call the probe's advantage averages \famGapRecentSem{} on the recent families against
\famGapEarlierSem{} on the others, and the largest advantage among the recent families,
\famRecentMaxSem{}, falls below the smallest among the others, \famEarlierMinSem{}. Runs are not
independent, since one checkpoint is evaluated on up to three benchmarks, so the unit for any test is the
checkpoint, of which there are \famNCkptSem{}, \famNCkptRecentSem{} recent and \famNCkptEarlierSem{}
earlier; they separate completely, the smallest attainable two-sided rank-test value at that size,
$p=\famCkptMannWhitneySem{}$. At the level of the family there are five observations and no test has power,
so we report the family means, Qwen3 \famGapQwenThreeSem{}, Gemma-3 \famGapGemmaThreeSem{} and Llama-3.2
\famGapLlamaThreeTwoSem{} against MiniCPM5 \famGapMiniCPMFiveSem{} and Qwen3.5 \famGapQwenThreeFiveSem{}. On
the call-expected items the recent families average \famGapRecentCall{} and confidence beats every trained
detector on \famConfWinsCall{} of \famNRecentCall{} such runs.

The obvious explanation fails. The recent families also fail far less often, and a probe needs labelled
failures while confidence needs none, so their probes might be starved. Thinning the labelled failures of the
Llama-1B run on BFCL from \curveBaseLlamaOneBBfclFullPos{} to \curveBaseLlamaOneBBfclLowPos{}, a tenfold
reduction, costs the probe \curveBaseLlamaOneBBfclDrop{} AUC, from \curveBaseLlamaOneBBfclFull{} to
\curveBaseLlamaOneBBfclLow{}, still far above the \confBaseLlamaOneBBfcl{} confidence reaches on the same
data. Across runs the rank correlation between labelled failures and the probe's advantage is
\famSpearmanSem{} ($p=\famSpearmanPSem{}$) and vanishes within each family, and Qwen3.5-0.8B supplies
\posQwenSmallBfclCount{} failures, more than most runs where the probe wins, with confidence still ahead. Nor
is it the summary: the best of nine confidence summaries narrows the probe's advantage on Llama-1B on BFCL
from \CvLlamaBfclGapMean{} to \CvLlamaBfclGapBest{} without closing it (Appendix~\ref{app:confvariants}). We
attribute the split to the family and, with five families, do not name the training property behind it; the
recent families emit calls in an XML dialect where the others emit JSON, and a forced-format control we have
not run would settle whether the surface form is the carrier. The practical consequence is firm: before
building an internal guardrail, score output confidence on a few hundred labelled calls from the model to be
deployed, because whether internals are needed cannot be inferred from size, attention design or release
date.

\subsection{Head resolution}
\label{sec:exp:perhead}
\label{sec:exp:ladder}

Every attention row of the detector grid reads the same tensors of the same generations under the same
splits; the blocks differ only in whether heads are averaged before the graph is built. The head-averaged
reads sit between \aucHeadAveragedLayerLlamaOneBGlaiveSem{} and \aucSymEigProfileLlamaThreeBGlaiveSem{} and on
several runs do not clear the length floor; the per-head profile reaches \aucPerHeadAllLlamaThreeBGlaiveSem{}
on Llama-3B, and over the \nRunsBothSpectral{} runs where both exist keeping heads apart is worth
\perHeadGainMean{} AUC points (\perHeadGainMin{} to \perHeadGainMax{}; the one negative entry is the
underpowered run). Propositions~\ref{prop:annihilation} and~\ref{prop:jensen} predict this but do not
guarantee that a failure is visible in the between-head contrast; the grid and the head-by-head view in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables} (Figure~\ref{fig:resolution}) establish that it is.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{fig3_ladder}
\caption{Resolution or width. Mean AUC over \ladNRuns{} runs, same stored features, classifier and splits, as
the number of heads kept apart increases (grey: individual runs). The two controls at the right hold the
per-head width but destroy head identity: averaged features padded with noise to the same width, and per-head
features with the head axis permuted independently per example.}
\label{fig:ladder}
\end{figure}

The per-head profile has $L\times H\times 5$ features and the averaged block $L\times 5$, so the comparison
varies two things at once; Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} separates them. Averaging the five metrics after computing
them per head, which removes the Jensen effect at unchanged width, scores \ladMetricAvg{} against
\ladGraphAvg{} for the averaged graph; keeping heads apart adds \ladGainPerHead{} on top, to \ladPerHead{}.
Padding the averaged features with noise to the per-head width leaves them at \ladNoisePadded{}, and
permuting the head axis independently for every example, which keeps every value and destroys only which head
produced it, drops the per-head profile to \ladHeadShuffled{}; random subsets of one, two, four and eight
heads score \ladOneHead{}, \ladTwoHeads{}, \ladFourHeads{} and \ladEightHeads{}, rising with the number kept.
The signal is carried by which heads do what, and a read that cannot tell them apart cannot see it, whatever
its width. Which of the five metrics leads changes with the run, so we report the five-metric profile rather
than a per-run best (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\subsection{The two attention families, and combining the two tiers}
\label{sec:exp:decorrelate}
\label{sec:exp:agreement}

Corollary~\ref{cor:decorrelate} makes three predictions. The first two hold: over \decorrNRuns{} runs the
Spearman correlation between the per-head and LapEigvals scores averages \decorrRhoMean{}
(\decorrRhoMin{} to \decorrRhoMax{}), and regressing each score on the other and scoring the residual gives
\decorrResidPerHead{} and \decorrResidLapEig{} AUC, so an in-degree sequence and a symmetrised spectrum carry
substantially independent evidence about the same call, as Remark~\ref{prop:lapeig} implies. The third
fails: concatenating the two feature sets changes AUC by \decorrUnionGain{} on average and improves on the
better one in \decorrUnionPositiveRuns{} of \decorrNRuns{} runs. The same holds across tiers. The residual
and attention judges are not redundant, yet stacking their out-of-fold scores with a fitted combiner changes
AUC by \agrStackGain{}, and within folds, where no pooling penalty exists, the average of the two judges
matches the better one and does not exceed it (\poolWithinMeanLlamaOneBGlaive{} against
\poolWithinPerHeadLlamaOneBGlaive{} on Llama-1B on Glaive). The rule an operator would reach for, flagging a
call only when both judges fire, appears to work and the appearance is an operating-point artefact: it fires
on fewer calls and so runs at lower recall, and against the stronger single judge at the recall it actually
achieves it changes precision by \agrMatchedGain{} and helps in \agrMatchedPositive{} of \agrNRuns{} runs. We
report this as a negative result without a mechanism we can defend (Appendix~\ref{app:combining}).

\subsection{Calls with a history}
\label{sec:exp:multiturn}

An agent's calls are made with a conversation behind them. We replay the multi-turn categories of BFCL-v4 with
the ground-truth calls of the two preceding turns in the prompt and ask for the current turn's calls; in the
\emph{cascade} condition one preceding turn carries a wrong call from another conversation, presented as
having succeeded (Appendix~\ref{app:multiturn}, Figure~\ref{fig:multiturn}). Two models, one from each side of
the family split, are run: MiniCPM5-2B on \mtNMiniCpm{} turns and Llama-3.2-1B on \mtNLlamaOneB{}, the latter a
boundary case that leaves \mtNegLlamaOneB{} correct calls, too few to train a judge on. A wrong call in the
history does not make
the next call worse: MiniCPM5 fails on \mtFailCleanMiniCpm{}\% after a clean history and
\mtFailCorruptMiniCpm{}\% after a corrupted one (Fisher $p=\mtFisherPMiniCpm{}$). Detection survives the
history: the token-role probe reaches \mtAucHiddenMiniCpm{}, the per-head profile \mtAucPerHeadMiniCpm{} and
LapEigvals \mtAucLapEigMiniCpm{} against a floor of \mtAucSurfaceMiniCpm{}, with the family split intact
(log-probability \mtAucLogprobMiniCpm{}, no trained judge above it). Trained on clean histories only, the probe
scores \mtAucHiddenCleanOnCleanMiniCpm{} on clean and \mtAucHiddenCorruptOnCleanMiniCpm{} on corrupted items,
the same as training on both (\mtAucHiddenCorruptOnAllMiniCpm{}): a corrupted history does not change what a
wrong call looks like from inside the model, so a judge's calibration set need not contain failed
trajectories. What a wrong tool \emph{result} does is a question this teacher-forced protocol cannot ask.

\paragraph{Cost.}
On Llama-3.2-1B, generation takes \latGen{}\,ms per call and the teacher-forced pass every internal judge
needs \latPass{}\,ms; on top of it the token-role features take under a millisecond, the LapEigvals column
sums \latLapEig{}\,ms and the per-head eigendecompositions \latPerHead{}\,ms, \latPerHeadPct{}\% of generation
(Appendix~\ref{app:cost}). Access, not compute, decides the tier.
