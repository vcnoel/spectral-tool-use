\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A hallucinated tool call can be judged from a single forward pass, and this study is about which signal to
read and when. First establish whether an internal judge is needed at all, because that depends on the model:
across the families we tested, either confidence is uninformative and a trained probe is the only thing that
works, or confidence is already as good as anything trained, and the two groups separate without being
explained by size, attention design, release date or label budget. Where internals are needed, a probe on
the residual stream at the structural positions of the call is the strongest judge, and attention weights
alone come within a few points of it, so an operator without activations still has an option. For attention,
one design choice decides everything: averaging over heads cannot represent the contrast between them and
can only overstate connectivity, a condition every layer we measured sits far from. Keeping the heads apart
is what moves the attention family from below a length baseline to within reach of the residual stream, and
resolution, not the choice of statistic, is what carries the signal.
