\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A hallucinated tool call can be judged from a single forward pass, and this study is about which signal to
read and when. First establish whether an internal judge is needed at all, because that depends on the model:
across the families we tested, either confidence is uninformative and a trained probe is the only thing that
works, or confidence is already as good as anything trained, and neither size, attention design, release
date nor label budget predicts which. Where internals are needed, a probe on the residual stream at the
structural positions of the call is the strongest judge, and attention weights alone come within a few
points of it. For attention, one design choice decides everything: averaging over heads cannot represent the
contrast between them and can only overstate connectivity. Keeping the heads apart moves the attention
family from below a length baseline to within reach of the residual stream; resolution, not the choice of
statistic, carries the signal.
