\section{Reading attention where internals are needed}
\label{sec:attention}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig3_attention}
\caption{The attention tier on every powered run: the length floor, the head-averaged spectral profile, the per-head profile, LapEigvals and the token-role probe. Keeping heads apart moves the attention readout from the floor to within a few points of the probe.}
\label{fig:attention}

An operator whose stack exposes attention weights but not activations wants to know how much of the probe an attention-only readout recovers. On the runs where internals are needed, the per-head spectral profile and LapEigvals trail the token-role probe by a few points (\cref{fig:attention}): \GapProbePerHeadLlamaOneBBfcl{} on Llama-3.2-1B and \GapProbePerHeadGemmaBfcl{} on Gemma-3-1B on BFCL, with the two attention readouts within noise of each other on every run (per-head minus LapEigvals \GapPerHeadLapEigLlamaOneBBfcl{} and \GapPerHeadLapEigGemmaBfcl{}). SinkProbe and the anchored readout of the call's own rows sit in the same band.

What decides whether an attention readout works at all is whether the heads are averaged before anything is computed. The head-averaged spectral profile, which is how most of this literature reads a layer, sits near the length floor on every run where internals are needed, within \havgFloorMinInternals{} to \havgFloorMaxInternals{} of it, and keeping heads apart adds \phHavgMinInternals{} to \phHavgMaxInternals{} on those runs. On the stored per-head features of \ladNRuns{} runs, averaging the five metrics over heads after computing them per head scores \ladMetricAvg{}, keeping them apart \ladPerHead{}, a gain of \ladGainPerHead{} that is positive on \ladGainPerHeadPos{} of \ladNRuns{} runs. Permuting the head axis independently for every item, which keeps every value and destroys only which head produced it, costs \ladGainVsShuffle{}, and padding the averaged features with noise to the per-head width changes them by \ladNoiseEffect{}. The signal is in which head does what.

The reason is known and we restate it for completeness in \cref{app:proofs}, following concurrent anonymous work. Averaging over heads is a many-to-one map, so no statistic of the averaged graph can tell apart head configurations that share a mean, and for the algebraic connectivity the averaged graph can only overstate the typical head, with equality when all heads share a Fiedler vector. On \jensenLayers{} layers of three models the averaged graph reports connectivity a median \jensenRatioMedianPct{}\% above the mean over heads. LapEigvals keeps heads apart, and its authors note that the causal Laplacian is triangular so its eigenvalues are its diagonal \citep{binkowski2025lapeigvals}: those features are a per-position degree sequence, while the symmetrised spectra read here are global functionals of the routing, and the two perform alike once heads are kept apart. The concurrent work also proves that relabeling-invariant summaries such as per-head spectra cannot represent which token attends to which and that a readout anchored on a distinguished row can. On tool calls the anchored readout of the call's own rows ties the per-head profile (\GapAnchPerHeadLlamaOneBBfcl{} and \GapAnchPerHeadGemmaBfcl{}), so that limit is not what separates attention from the residual stream on this task.
