A difference between model families can come from the items, the labels, the prompt or the extraction. Each paragraph below changes one thing and keeps the rest of the protocol fixed. The difficulty, budget and summary controls were run on every scored failure, before the decomposition by kind of error.

\paragraph{Item difficulty.}
A model that fails rarely fails on hard items, and its confidence may register hardness instead of its own error. On BFCL an item's difficulty is the share of the other checkpoints that fail it. That share alone predicts a model's failures at \diffOnlyMin{} to \diffOnlyMax{} AUC. Scoring both detectors inside strata of difficulty removes what either gains from knowing which items are hard, and the split does not close (\cref{fig:controls}a). The advantage is \diffGapWithinLlamaOneB{} on Llama-3.2-1B and \diffGapWithinLlamaThreeB{} on Llama-3.2-3B, and between \diffGapWithinQwenThreeFive{} and \diffGapWithinQwenThree{} on the three confidence-side checkpoints. Confidence on the confidence side tracks the model's own errors beyond the hardness of the request.

\paragraph{Label budget.}
A probe needs labelled failures and confidence needs none, and the confidence-side models fail less often. Training the Llama-3.2 probes on as few failures as the other families supply, with the test folds untouched, costs them \budgetProbeDropLlamaOneB{} AUC at 1B and \budgetProbeDropLlamaThreeB{} at 3B (\cref{fig:controls}b). At \budgetMinPosLlamaOneB{} training failures the advantage is still \budgetGapAtMinLlamaOneB{} and \budgetGapAtMinLlamaThreeB{}, above every value on the other side.

\paragraph{The confidence summary.}
The mean log-probability averages over every token of the call, while the probe reads the argument values separately, so a summary restricted to the value tokens could do better. Only \nMultiSummaryInternals{} of the \nInternalsRuns{} probe-side runs store several summaries, three of them over the call's own tokens, and none stores one over the value tokens alone. On these two runs the summary with the highest AUC on the other folds, applied fold by fold, moves the advantage to \gapBestLlamaOneBBfcl{} on Llama-3.2-1B and \gapBestGemmaBfcl{} on Gemma-3, and an oracle that picks the best summary on the test items leaves \gapOracleLlamaOneBBfcl{} and \gapOracleGemmaBfcl{} (\cref{fig:controls}c, \cref{app:confidence}). On the confidence side the fold-chosen advantage is at most \gapBestConfidenceMax{}.

\paragraph{Benchmark and schema echoes.}
On the probe side, Gemma-3 reads \GapGemmaGlaive{} on Glaive and \GapGemmaBfcl{} on BFCL and Llama-3.2-1B keeps its sign on three benchmarks. The confidence side has powered runs on BFCL and BFCL-live only, so its benchmark breadth is two. Keeping the first occurrence of each unique Glaive request gives \gdGapLlamaOneBGlaive{} on Llama-3.2-1B, \gdGapGemmaGlaive{} on Gemma-3 and \gdGapLlamaThreeBGlaive{} on Llama-3.2-3B. With matched schema echoes removed from evaluation, the pooled advantage keeps its sign on \nNoEchoHolds{} of the \nInternalsRuns{} probe-side runs. The exception is Llama-3.2-1B on the live categories, which moves from \GapLlamaOneBLive{} to \GapNoEchoLlamaOneBLive{} [\GapNoEchoLoLlamaOneBLive{}, \GapNoEchoHiLlamaOneBLive{}]. Its value-error advantage is also near zero, so this run is on neither side once format failures are set aside.

\paragraph{Extraction provenance.}
The first extractor produced \nFirstExtractInternals{} of the \nInternalsRuns{} probe-side runs and \nFirstExtractConfidence{} of the \nConfidenceRuns{} confidence-side runs, so extractor version lines up with side. The one run extracted under both reads \replGapStale{} and \replGapClean{}, a drift of \replGapDelta{}, small against the probe-side advantages and not small against single-run differences on the confidence side. Differences below this drift are not interpreted.

The permutation null of the probe pipeline, the fold-level gaps and the protocol choices that move the numbers are in \cref{app:more}.
